# Asset Pricing

Once we understand consumption and saving, the next question is how the saving gets
allocated across assets, and what the resulting demand implies about prices. This
part runs the argument in both directions.

It begins with the portfolio problem taken in isolation. Under CARA utility the solution
is a dollar amount held in the risky asset, independent of wealth; under CRRA utility the
Merton-Samuelson result instead makes the risky *share* independent of wealth. The logic
then reverses. Lucas takes consumption as exogenous and solves for the price that makes
households willing to hold the market. With many assets, the same reasoning gives the
consumption CAPM; applied to data, it gives the equity premium puzzle. No plausible degree
of risk aversion explains the historical excess return on equity: matching it in postwar
U.S. data takes a coefficient of relative risk aversion of at least 15 to 20, and that only
if consumption and stock returns moved in lockstep, while the riskless rate in the same data
wants one of at most 2/3.

The part closes by asking what prices are consistent with the absence of arbitrage
even when they diverge from fundamentals.
