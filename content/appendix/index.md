# Mathematical Background

The results collected here get used repeatedly in the main chapters, but deriving them
where they are first needed would interrupt the argument.

Two of them concern approximation: how to replace a continuous lognormal distribution
with a small number of equiprobable points, in the univariate and then the bivariate
case, so that a computer can evaluate expectations by simple averaging. The rest is
reference material. It covers the conditions under which the behavior of many
heterogeneous households collapses into a single aggregate relationship, a side-by-side
comparison of the discrete and continuous time formulations of the Ramsey problem, and a
list of the mathematical facts this literature leans on most often.
