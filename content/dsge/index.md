# DSGE Models

Adding productivity shocks to the growth model turns a theory of accumulation into a
theory of business cycles. Almost nothing in that literature has a closed form solution.

The Brock-Mirman case is the exception. Logarithmic utility, Cobb-Douglas production, and
complete depreciation within the period together yield an exact policy rule, which makes
this model worth studying closely: it is the benchmark against which numerical solutions
to harder problems can be checked. The Prescott real business cycle model gives up that
luxury in exchange for realism, and asks how much of observed output volatility
technology shocks alone can account for.
