\begin{verbatimwrite}{\jobname.title}
The Hall-Jorgenson Model of Investment
\end{verbatimwrite}

\handoutNameMake
\begin{verbatimwrite}{./body.tex}
\cite{hall&jorgenson:i} consider the problem of a firm that produces output 
using capital $\kap$ as its only input,
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
       \inc  & =  \fFunc(\kap)
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}
and which obtains its capital $\kap$ from a market in which a unit
of capital can be rented for a unit of time at rate 
 $\kapRent_{t}$.   

In period $t$, the firm maximizes profit,\opt{MarginNotes}{\marginpar{\tiny This implicitly normalizes the price of output to 1.}}
\begin{displaymath}
        \max_{\kap_{t}} ~ \kap_{t}^{\kapShare}-\kapRent_{t}\kap_{t}
\end{displaymath}
yielding first order conditions
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
\fFunc^{\prime}(k_{t}) & =  \kapRent_{t}
\\        \kapShare \kap_{t}^{\kapShare-1} & =  \kapRent_{t}  \\
         (\inc_{t}/\kap_{t}) \kapShare & =  \kapRent_{t}  \\
        \kap_{t} & =  (\inc_{t}/\kapRent_{t})\kapShare . \label{eq:khj}
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}
\opt{MarginNotes}{\marginpar{\tiny This equation says level of capital is always instantly adjusted to $\inc_{t}$ and $\kapRent_{t}$ - no costs of adjustment.}}

What determines the cost of capital?  In the simple case with no taxes and no 
capital market frictions of any kind, an investor must be indifferent 
between putting his money in the bank and earning interest at rate 
$\rfree$, and buying a unit of capital, renting it out at rate $\kapRent_{t}$, and 
then reselling it the next period.

The price at which capital goods can be bought at date $t$ is: \opt{MarginNotes}{\marginpar{\tiny Purchase price is distinct from rental rate - much larger.}}
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \Price_{t} & - & \text{purchase price of one unit of capital},
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}
and in continuous time, the rate of change of $\Price_{t}$ is $\dot{\Price}_{t}$.  Assume
that capital depreciates geometrically at rate $\depr$.  The net profit
from the {\it continuous time} purchase-and-rent strategy is
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \kapRent_{t}-\depr \Price_{t}+\dot{\Price}_{t} & - & \text{Income from renting, minus loss from depreciation}  \\
         &   \text{plus capital gain from the change in price of capital}. \nonumber
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}

\thispagestyle{empty}

Thus, the no-arbitrage condition is \opt{MarginNotes}{\marginpar{\tiny LHS is what you get from putting money in a bank at rate $\rfree$, which we assume is perfectly certain and time-invariant.}}
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \rfree \Price_{t} & =  \kapRent_{t} - \depr \Price_{t} + \dot{\Price}_{t} 
\\  (\rfree+\depr) \Price_{t} & =  \kapRent_{t}+\dot{\Price}_{t}     \label{eq:ctarb}.
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}

Now to simplify our lives we will assume constant capital goods 
prices, $\dot{\Price}_{t}=0$.  Thus, substituting the value for $\kapRent_{t}$ from
\eqref{eq:ctarb} into \eqref{eq:khj} we have:
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \kap_{t} & =  \kapShare \inc_{t}/\kapRent_{t}  \\
         & =  \kapShare \inc_{t}/(\rfree+\depr)\Price_{t}.
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}


Now let's introduce taxes, defined as follows:\opt{MarginNotes}{\marginpar{\tiny Apologize for stupidity of $k$ as ITC.  Note also that if the tax treatment of depreciation does not change over time, then $z_{t}=z$ is a constant; we will assume it is constant henceforth.}}
\begin{equation*}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \taxCorp & - & \text{corporate tax rate ($\approx 0.34$ in US) }
\\      \itc & - & \text{investment tax credit (sometimes 10 percent, sometimes 0) }
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation*}

The net, discounted, after-tax price of capital to the firm is\footnote{Assume that if the firm sells the capital, it must repay the ITC on a pro-rata basis; this prevents tax arbitrage opportunities.}
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \hat{\Price}_{t} & =  (1-\itc) \Price_{t}.
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}

Now let's rewrite the arbitrage equation \eqref{eq:ctarb} taking account of taxes: \opt{MarginNotes}{\marginpar{\tiny Must multiply $\kapRent_{t}$ by $(1-\taxCorp)$ because the capital-rental business must pay taxes too.}}
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        (\rfree+\depr) \hat{\Price}_{t} & =  (1-\taxCorp) \kapRent_{t}+\dot{\hat{\Price}}_{t}.
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}

If we simplify again by assuming that $\dot{\hat{\Price}}_{t}=0$, we have
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \kapRent_{t} & =  (\rfree+\depr)\Price_{t}(1-\itc)/(1-\taxCorp)
.
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}

Note that so far we have not derived a formula for investment - we 
have derived a formula for the {\it level} of the capital stock.  But 
net investment is just the difference between the capital stock in 
periods $t$ and $t-1$.  Thus, the Hall-Jorgenson model of gross investment is
\begin{equation}\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}
        \inv_{t-1} & =  \kap_{t}-\kap_{t-1}+\depr \kap_{t-1}  \\
         & =  \left(\Delta \frac{\inc_{t}}{\kapRent_{t}}\right)\kapShare+\depr \kap_{t-1}
\end{aligned}\end{gathered}\end{equation}
(where we neglect some minor complications having to do with the distinction between
continuous and discrete time).

\end{verbatimwrite}
